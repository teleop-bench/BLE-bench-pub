GATT duplex: FSU converts once the interval leaves the saturated regime

Bluetooth 6.0's Frame Space Update does nothing for two-way (duplex) traffic at short intervals — the event is already full — but beyond that the freed airtime does convert, peaking at 25 ms. Aggregate throughput (A→B + B→A), drift-cancelled ABBA.

FSU on (52 µs tIFS) FSU off (150 µs) FSU dividend ±1 SD (n=4; 37.5/50 ms n=8)

Reading it: 15 ms is neutral (+1%, lines overlap — no FSU headroom in a saturated short event). 25 ms is the decisive peak (+11.9%, well separated). 37.5 and 50 ms stay positive (+6.4% / +9.3%), and the +6.4% at 37.5 ms reproduced across two ABBA blocks (n=8) so the effect is solid — but those two are within measurement noise of each other (per-point SD ~3–5%), so don't read 50 > 37.5 as real. The dividend is non-monotonic, not a smooth ramp: FSU frees a fixed airtime slice per packet that only converts when it accumulates to a whole extra packet, so the %-gain is quantized (25 ms lands on a clean packet boundary; 37.5 ms falls just short). Bottom line: ~0 in the saturated regime, real and meaningful (+6–12%) beyond it.

Method: open Zephyr GATT duplex (central blast + peripheral echo), aggregate = central echo-received + peripheral blast-received, steady-window medians, ABBA order (off,on,on,off,off,on,on,off) drift-cancelled. n=4 per point at 15/25 ms; n=8 (two pooled ABBA blocks) at 37.5/50 ms. Whiskers ±1 SD. 2× nRF54L15, 2M PHY. Evidence: debug-evidence/duplex-fsu-abba-20260824/. Same-bench FSU effect; absolute KB/s are RF-day-specific. 1 KB = 1024 B.
